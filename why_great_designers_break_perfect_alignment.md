# Your Eyes Don't Do Math

## 1 The Human Perception Gap

Mathematical alignment places elements perfectly based on grids, spacing and exact measurements. But optical alignment focuses on how the design feels visually balanced to the human eye.

## 2 Youtube's Play Button

A triangle feels visually heavier on its flat side than its pointed tip. So if the play icon is mathematically centered, it actually looks slightly left-heavy. That's why designers push it a little to the right so it feels perfectly balanced to the eye.

## 3 The Curve Illusion

Curved letters like O, G, and C naturally appear smaller than flat letters. So if every letter is mathematically kept at the exact same height, the curved ones feel visually weaker. That's why designer slightly extend curved letters above and below the guidelines. 

## 4. The Irradiation Phenomenon

Light shape on dark backgrounds naturally appear thicker and larger to our eyes. So if Apple used the exact same logo weight for both black and white versions, the while logo would look visually heavier. That's why brands slightly out while logos on dark backgrounds to keep the balance visually consistent. 

## 5. The Gravity Effect

When a smaller object is placed at the exact mathematical center, our eyes often perceive it as slightly dropping downward. This is called the gravity effect. That's why designers slightly raise the inner element above the true center, so the composition feels visually balanced and stable.
